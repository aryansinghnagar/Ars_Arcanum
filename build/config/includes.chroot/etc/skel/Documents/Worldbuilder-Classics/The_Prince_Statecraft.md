# The Prince: Faction Dynamics & Sovereign Rule

## XVII. Cruelty and Clemency: Better to be Feared than Loved
1. Every prince ought to desire to be considered clement and not cruel. Nevertheless, he ought to take care not to misuse this clemency.
2. A prince, so long as he keeps his subjects united and loyal, ought not to mind the reproach of cruelty; because with a few examples he will be more merciful than those who, through too much mercy, allow disorders to arise, from which follow murders or robberies.
3. Upon this a question arises: whether it is better to be loved than feared, or the reverse?
   - The answer is that one would wish to be both, but, because it is difficult to unite them in one person, it is much safer to be feared than loved, when, of the two, either must be dispensed with.
   - For men generally are ungrateful, fickle, false, cowardly, and greedy. As long as you succeed they are entirely yours, but when necessity comes upon you, they revolt.
4. Nevertheless, a prince ought to inspire fear in such a way that, if he does not win love, he avoids hatred; because he can endure very well being feared whilst he is not hated.

## XVIII. In What Way Princes Should Keep Faith
1. How laudable it is for a prince to keep his word and live with integrity and not by cunning, everyone knows; nevertheless, one sees by experience in our times that those princes who have done great things have held good faith of little account.
2. You must know there are two ways of contesting: the one by the law, the other by force; the first method is proper to men, the second to beasts; but because the first is frequently not sufficient, it is necessary to have recourse to the second.
3. Therefore, a prince ought to know how to use both the beast and the man:
   - He must imitate the **fox** and the **lion**: the lion cannot defend himself on snares and the fox cannot defend himself against wolves. One must therefore be a fox to discover snares and a lion to terrify wolves.
